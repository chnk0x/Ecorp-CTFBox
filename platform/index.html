01 / Incident briefing

One breach. Six evidence trails.

Designed as a guided CTF for cybersecurity learners: each solved stage reveals the next piece of the fictional E Corp incident.

Start with the evidence, not assumptions.

Inspect a suspicious email, recover a clue hidden in an image, decode an attacker message, and investigate a deliberately vulnerable employee portal. Then trace a network capture and finish on a Linux host.

Stages unlock in order. Submit the recovered flag through the board to record a solve and continue the chain.

All names, accounts, and incident data are fictional.

03 / How the lab is built

A small stack, doing real work.

The interface reflects the project that actually runs: one Flask platform and two interactive challenge services, with downloadable evidence for the other stages.

Players use the browser-based board. Evidence for EC-01, EC-02, EC-03 and EC-05 is released as each stage unlocks.

05 / Field notes

Before you dive in.

A few practical details about progress, hints and the environment.

How does a stage unlock?

Stages follow a fixed chain. A correct flag is checked by the server; solving the current stage unlocks the next stage’s evidence or access card.

How is progress recorded?

Accounts, solves and hint costs are recorded by the platform. The scoreboard updates after a player submits their first accepted flag.

How do hints affect score?

Each stage has three optional hints. The first costs zero; later hint costs vary by challenge and are shown before you request them.

What flag format should I expect?

Submit the complete flag recovered from that stage. The server trims surrounding whitespace and compares it without revealing the stored answer to the browser.

Where are the interactive targets?

Access details are revealed by the EC-04 and EC-06 stage cards when you reach them in the challenge sequence.

What is in scope?

Only the fictional systems identified in this CTF are in scope. Do not test the techniques against unrelated systems.
